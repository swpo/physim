Worlds / Field dynamics

Structures made of fields

Blobs are structures maintained by interacting fields. They are useful physical objects precisely because the simulator does not prescribe their trajectories.

An activator amplifies local disturbances, while coupled channels feed back over different distances and timescales. Diffusion spreads those effects. In suitable parameter ranges, the balance supports a localized spot with a surrounding field profile. Other settings produce motion, stripes, splitting, or decay.

The field model

For the worked world, activators ui and channels xc follow this form:

∂t uᵢ = Dᵢ ∇²uᵢ + λᵢuᵢ − uᵢ³ + kᵢ − Σc Kᵢc x꜀ + noiseᵢ
∂t x꜀ = D꜀ ∇²x꜀ + (drive꜀(u) − x꜀) / τ꜀

The genome defines the coefficients and channel drives. The p4g2_044 genome has four activators, eight channels, and no bilinear interaction terms. The broader generator can also construct genomes with bilinear terms. Fields may be signed; they are not all conserved concentrations.

A world's behavior depends on its numerical realization as well as its equations. Grid spacing, boundary conditions, timestep, precision, and noise conventions belong in its reproducibility record. See the simulator reference.

From spots to interactions

A three-field model provides a simpler foundation: one activator and two inhibitory channels can support persistent spots. Published work on dissipative solitons establishes the physical background for spot existence, drift, and bound states. The Physim research examples explore particular realizations of these effects and constructions built from them.

Selected systems also support composite motion, rotation, and transport. These results motivate a diverse world collection. A successful construction in one system does not imply that the current prediction suite measures that capability in another.

The worked world

In p4g2_044, spots and stripes coexist with negative defects on positive field backgrounds. Observed source responses include nonlinear dose effects and changes that depend on probe position. The evaluation uses controlled pulses, repeated interventions, and probe placement to make parts of those responses measurable.

Terms such as “species”, “organism”, and “cell” sometimes appear in research descriptors. They refer to operational classifications of field structures, not claims of biological life.

Scientific background

Schenk, Or-Guil, Bode & Purwins, Physical Review Letters 78, 3781 (1997), introduced the foundational three-component dissipative-soliton model. Purwins, Bödeker & Amiranashvili, Advances in Physics 59, 485 (2010), reviews dissipative solitons. These are background for the small-system examples; the evolved multi-field genomes are separate model instances.
