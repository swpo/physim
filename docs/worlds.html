Blob-field worlds

A world is a set of field dynamics. A prepared instance fixes where an experiment begins and how its instruments are arranged.

Fields assign a value to every point on a two-dimensional periodic grid. Local interactions, diffusion, and ongoing disturbances evolve those values. A “blob” is a persistent localized structure in the fields. Its movement and interactions emerge from the dynamics. Read more about field dynamics.

What the instruments reveal

Two movable probe arrays sample all anonymous ports at multiple positions: 12 in the worked example above, four in the prepared BF laboratory, and six in XV. A global sensor reports each port's spatial mean and variance. A fixed source injects through one port at a time. Moving a probe changes its readings without moving the emitter or disturbing the fields.

Each trial restores the same prepared fields and apparatus, then draws independent ongoing noise. The investigator can therefore compare treatments and repeat experiments, while still needing to represent uncertainty.

A wider space of physical systems

World generation changes the number of activators and channels, their timescales, diffusion, and couplings. Earlier small systems provide interpretable examples of persistent spots, motion, binding, and transport. Larger evolved systems combine several kinds of structure. These phenomena belong to particular parameter settings; they are not properties of every world.
