Measuring evolved worlds: the probe-device benchmark

Post 11 — the evolution campaign (post 10) left an archive of 423 worlds nobody understands. This post is the benchmark that turns the best of them into a measurement problem for AI scientists — and its current results. First published as the setup edition; updated with the clean-slate contract system (BLOB2) and the round-4 results, and restructured current-first: how evaluation works now is sections 1–4; the rounds-1–3 path that produced it is compressed into section 5. Code: environments/physim/ (the environment) · probes/blobs/agentenv/ (instrument + adequacy). Design doc: probes/blobs/l0/deepsearch/TRACKA_CLEANSLATE_EVAL.md · interface history: TRACKA_R2_CONTROLS.md · results: probes/blobs/agentenv/round4/ROUND4_FINAL.md.

The benchmark, now. A frontier coding agent is dropped end-to-end into an evolved reaction–diffusion world it is told nothing about — no fields, no images, no equations, not even the words "blob" or "lattice". The roster: fable-5 (Anthropic) under the claude_code harness and gpt-5.6-sol (OpenAI) under codex, three rollouts per (model, world), docker runtime, the world served as MCP tools; notebook, files, and offline compute come free with the harness. The agent's one sense organ is a probe device whose structure is real but never documented (section 1). It gets a budgeted span of passive observation to a hard stop, then live replica forks for causal experiments — and it must submit predictions on a world-adaptive contract menu, the capability ladder L1–L4D (section 2). Every contract is scored as skill against an evaluator-side baseline ladder; an unsubmitted contract scores −1; episode reward is mean skill over the menu. Two eval worlds ship: BLOB2-E1 (p4g2_044 — 21 sparse stable blobs) and BLOB2-E2 (p6g8_033 — the campaign champion, a reorganizing labyrinth with rotors). A scripted reference actor plays the same episodes through the same tools; its per-contract skill ships with the environment as the published floor agents must beat (section 3).

Headline (round 4, n=3 rollouts per model×world). fable-5 is the first agent above the scripted floor on both worlds: +0.308 on BLOB2-E1 and +0.287 on BLOB2-E2, vs the published floor's +0.238 / +0.233. gpt-5.6-sol is negative on every rollout of both worlds. The lead is built on the tiers where the script is honestly weak, the dose leg is where agents shine (L4D spikes to 0.99), the hidden-sensor tier is dented but not cracked, and n=3 is a floor, not a book — tables and readings in section 4.

1 · The instrument: a probe device

Evolved worlds are particulate — the interesting objects are localized, mobile, and sparse (21 stable blobs in a 128×128-unit box on E1). The fixed anonymous point taps every earlier physim tier used almost surely sit in vacuum there, and the natural actions ("poke it", "push a blob in") are spatial — a point-force interface cannot even express them. So the instrument became spatial without ever disclosing space. The design principle (settled 2026-08-31) is "learn to use your hands," applied to eyes: scientists are not told their retina's wiring; they wave a hand and calibrate. Discovering your own sensor geometry is the first experiment of every episode. Structure exists; it is consistent; it is not explained.

One device = k point sensors in a rigid local patch around a movable center, sampling every world field bilinearly (continuum-honest — no grid reads), plus a co-located emitter at the center. The agent gets, per device:

controlsurfacewhat's hidden
streamsk × n_ports anonymous scalar channels @ 5 tu node layout (square-13 / hex-19 / …), node order (secretly permuted), port identities (secretly permuted per world)
actuator3 anonymous channels u ∈ [−1,1]³, cost |u₁|+|u₂|+|u₃| per step, charged as commanded what the channels do: two translate the device, one scales its sensor spacing — in a fixed global convention that is simply never documented (undocumented IS the mechanism, exactly like the fixed output retinotopy: nature wires actuators once, mastery transfers across worlds, and the WORLD is what varies). Spacing has undisclosed bounds; a step that would cross one is refused with a generic adjust_rejected — and still costs its commanded effort (strain), so the refusal is not a free bound-measuring oracle. Pure-translation steps are never refused
emissioninject(port, amp, dur) through one fixed emission channel, budgetedthe emitter's location and coupling (post-round-1 revision: originally disclosed as co-located with a device; now fully undisclosed — localizing it from decaying transients is intended science, and fable-5 did exactly that in round 1), the stamp profile (σ=2 Gaussian), what the port drives
global statsfree per-port mean/variance of the whole mediumeverything spatial — it's a weather report, not a map

The only structural facts an agent ever sees are counts: 2 devices, 13 and 19 slots, 12 ports, 3 actuator channels. Everything else — that slots form a lattice, that the two devices differ in geometry, what each actuator channel does, that the box wraps — is discoverable, priced science.

The barrier, stated once. The genome→environment boundary is the program's oldest law (post 7 onward: searchers never see genomes). Here it means: (b1) ports are a secret permutation of the world's fields — never named; (b2) instruments are generic signal processing; (b3) contracts are defined ONLY on sensor-observable quantities — no window counts, no positions, nothing that presumes geometry we refuse to disclose; (b4) the env code path touches the sim state only through the port layer (blob metrics stay on the evaluator side); (b5) worlds come from the archive by published rule, contracts are fixed per (world, seed). The environment's test suite greps every agent-visible string — tool responses, prompts, docstrings, contract announcements — against a leak-pattern list (coordinates, lattice words, port names, blob words). It runs in CI with the other gates.

Episode machinery

worlds : BLOB2-E1 = p4g2_044 (21 sparse stable blobs) · BLOB2-E2 = p6g8_033 (reorganizing labyrinth + rotors)
roster : device 0 = square-13 (witness/anchor) · device 1 = hex-19 (stronger instrument) · ~18–30 u apart, undisclosed
span   : t = 0 → 1,700 tu at 5 tu steps, passive replay of the cached trajectory — then a HARD STOP (T0)
replicas: after the span, up to 12 live forks from the span-end state: inject(port, amp ≤ 1.0, dur) through the fixed emission channel, read at chosen lags ≤ 250 tu
budgets : sensor 40,000 node·tu (≈½ duty for the 32-slot pair) · adjust 1,200 cu · injection 120 amp·tu at price amp·(1+4·max(0, amp−0.5))

The budgets are not arbitrary: the A0 adequacy study priced them before any agent ran — 54 scripted-pipeline cells (3 worlds × 3 seeds × 3 budget tiers × 2 device rosters) that located the capability-vs-budget knee. Episodes run at 2× A0's baseline tier, ON the steep part of the curve: geometry self-calibration survives, the motion basis is expensive, nothing survives 1/16 duty — so budget skill is real skill (probes/blobs/agentenv/ADEQUACY.md). The amp-1.0 emission cap with convex pricing is also an A0 finding: the announced amp-3.0 response is a free beacon (z-scores 600–10,000), so predicting it must be done by calibrating below the cap and extrapolating — response science, not response reading.

The hard stop is the information structure: everything after t=1700 is exactly what the contracts ask about, so it is never directly observable. The replica mechanism prices causal experimentation separately from passive observation — and because replicas share the start state and noise stream, differences between them are pure causal response.

The agent-facing surface

probe_status()             → time, budgets, prices, caps, contracts, locks
probe_read_streams(window, …) → advance + read (slots × 5 node·tu per device per read step)
probe_wait(steps)          → advance free, no reads
probe_adjust(device, u1, u2, u3) → anonymous 3-channel actuator, costed as commanded
probe_inject(port, amp, dur, lags, …) → ONE replica fork per call, priced
probe_submit(contract, payload) → mean+sigma arrays; last submission scores

Notebook, files, and offline compute come free with the coding harness — the agent is expected to dump every read to disk and model offline, exactly like the C-track scientists did. Budgets are enforced server-side; a rejected action costs nothing but a turn.

2 · The contract ladder (BLOB2)

The contract system is a clean-slate design (TRACKA_CLEANSLATE_EVAL.md): "understanding a world" decomposes into gradeable capabilities — ontology, dynamics, causality, transfer, economy — under three grading rules. Never grade descriptions (language is gameable); grade the predictive consequences of having the right description. Contracts are the curriculum: unpriced capabilities go unexercised, so the contract set must span the capabilities or agents rationally skip them. And score skill, not raw accuracy, so world difficulty cancels and "beating the bar" is explicit per contract. Section 5 tells what those rules cost to learn; this is the system that runs:

Skill-normalized scoring

skill(contract) = clip( 1 − CRPS_agent / CRPS_best_baseline , −1, +1 )
baseline ladder = climatology · persistence · AR(2) — pose-ignorant, full-rate pre-span information, computed evaluator-side at scoring time; AR(2) enters where it applies (the stream forecast); the per-contract ladder values are published in every result, never shown to agents
unsubmitted contract → skill = −1  ·  episode reward = mean skill over the world's menu

Every submission is a mean + honest sigma per target; the metric is CRPS. Zero means "matched the best rung of the baseline ladder" on every contract regardless of world difficulty; +1 is the clip ceiling (CRPS → 0); −1 is the clip floor and the price of skipping, so the curriculum cannot be dodged. The scripted actor is not in the formula: the zero of the scale is the baseline ladder, while the actor is a published reference row (section 3) — the floor an agent must beat, not the bar it is normalized against. Forecast tiers (L1/L2/L3F/L3E/L3S) lock at the first injection — they are forecasts issued on span information; the response tiers stay open all episode.

World-adaptive menus, two worlds

Fixed menus misprice heterogeneous worlds (E1 needs little geometry; E2 punishes its absence). The evaluator knows each world's certified phenomenology from the assay records — species counts, spatial class, motion class, timescales — so the menu is generated per world: every certified phenomenon gets at least one contract that prices it, and no contract references phenomena the world lacks. The selection is evaluator-side knowledge only; the menu discloses nothing beyond what any contract necessarily discloses about what is measurable.

Both tags are harder than rounds 1–3 even where the world is the same: the hidden sensor and the dose leg have no fixed-pose shortcut, an unsubmitted contract is −1 instead of 0, the system prompt lost its "suggested science" hints (round-3 evidence: fable follows its own program anyway; hints anchor and leak our framing of what is interesting), and the whole surface re-passed an extended barrier audit (zero pose/location/actuator-semantics language, the L2 wording especially). Episode machinery, budgets, and the amp-1.0 cap are unchanged from section 1.

Spend accounting and the dose leg

Budgets are enforced server-side; a rejected action costs nothing but a turn. What an episode spends is recorded per rollout as report-only conduct metrics — the spent fraction of each budget (sensor / adjust / injection), replica count, turn count — published alongside the scores but never mixed into them (conduct is a trait, not a reward term; the physim-wide rule). The injection budget is where the accounting bites: the price amp·(1+4·max(0, amp−0.5)) under the amp-1.0 cap means L4's announced amp-3.0 protocol can never be run directly — an agent must spend replicas on a sub-cap calibration ladder, fit the dose law, and extrapolate. L4D then pays for exactly that law at an amp nobody announced: the budget system and the contract system price the same capability from both sides.

Two more tiers are specified but not yet priced: L5 preparation ("drive an observable into a band and hold it" — the classic prep-track ported) and L6 executable theory (a compact predictor submitted as code, scored on held-out seeds and poses, size-capped). They phase into later rounds.

3 · Gates: the meter is measured first

Every physim instrument is tested before the agents are (post 2's telescope fix, post 9's parity gates); this one has three layers, all in CI. The barrier audit: the test suite greps every agent-visible string against a leak-pattern list (section 1). The null-harness gate: a scripted actor — the A0 informed pipeline re-expressed against the MCP tool surface (burst reads at ~½ duty, duty-corrected event rates, a control replica plus sub-cap calibration emissions, linear templates) — must reproduce adequacy-grade skill through the agent tools alone, without touching a single internal API; when the pattern was established in round 1, its amp-0 control replica came out bitwise identical to the cached A0 control branch. And the gate suites: the legacy six plus round-2 G1–G6 (menu registry, L1 pose/truth parity through the agent surface, dose-interpolation exactness, skill clipping, the extended barrier audit, submit/locks).

The same scripted actor, run on BLOB2 (smoke_blob2.py), is the published floor — part of the benchmark, shipped with the environment:

Readings, in order of confidence:

The bar is cleared, on both worlds. fable-5 beats the published scripted floor by +0.07 on E1 and +0.05 on E2 — the first positive-skill agent result on the clean-slate system. The margin is modest, and it comes from the right place: on the tiers where the script is already strong (L4/L4D) the per-tier means stay below the script's rows, and the lead is built where the script is honestly weak (L3F +0.26 vs −0.06 on E1; L3S +0.47 vs −0.29 and L2 +0.20 vs −0.00 on E2).

The dose leg is where agents shine. L4/L4D are fable's strongest tiers everywhere, with L4D spiking to 0.99 in one rollout per world — the calibrate-below-the-cap, fit-the-law, interpolate strategy executed at agent quality. Law-learning is the capability the redesign added, and it is the one agents deliver on first.

L2 is dented, not cracked. The hidden-sensor nowcast — the tier built to price spatial world-modeling — is still ~0 on E1 in all three rollouts; on E2 two of three rollouts posted 0.28. Partial skill means a partial spatial model exists. The open-headroom tier is being dented, not cracked.

Flat spots. L3E stayed flat-to-negative on E1 (−0.02 — well above the script's −0.55, still no skill), and L1 is only modestly positive on both worlds: the ladder now pays for actuator science, and after the zero-actuator rounds that much works — but calibration quality is ordinary.

Variance is the caveat. E2 rollouts span 0.16 → 0.45. n=3 is a floor, not a book: the defensible sentence is "fable-5 clears the scripted bar on both worlds", not any per-tier ranking beyond the spikes above.

And the sol row is a result, not a footnote: negative on every rollout of both worlds under a rule where matching the best baseline-ladder rung scores 0 and an unsubmitted contract costs −1. The harness gap that rounds 1 and 3 exposed (0.08/0.05 vs fable's 0.689/0.632) survives the redesign intact.

5 · How the instrument got here (rounds 1–3, compressed)

The system above is the second design. The first (rounds 1–3, tags BLOB-E1→BLOB-E1r3, world p4g2_044 only) ran a three-contract menu ported from physim's point-tap tracks — P3 injection response (weight 0.5, the flagship), P2 event counts (0.3), P1′ short-horizon streams (0.2), scored as CRPS against scripted reference baselines. Same instrument, same episode machinery. It produced the roster's first scores — and the two findings that retired it:

roundsurfacefable-5gpt-5.6-solscripted actor
1BLOB-E1 — documented semantic controls (move/dilate), emitter co-location disclosed0.6890.080.60
3BLOB-E1r3 — one opaque 3-channel probe_adjust, emitter undisclosed0.6320.050.60*

*the scripted actor never adjusts, so its round-1 row stays valid on r3 (exemption recorded in the scorecard). Round 2's intermediate surface — secretly mixed control channels — was killed in flight: mixing is difficulty without depth; nothing from it was scored.

Finding 1 — the interface was leaking. Round 1's probe_move/probe_dilate tools disclosed, through API shape alone, that the pose space factorizes into two translation axes plus a zoom — and the docs disclosed the emitter's co-location. The audit (TRACKA_R2_CONTROLS.md) replaced them with the opaque probe_adjust surface of section 1; re-running fable on it priced the leak at ~0.06 of score (0.689 → 0.632) — points that had been bought by the API shape, not by science through the streams.

Finding 2 — zero actuator use in anger across all 12 scored rollouts of rounds 1+3 (one u1-identification test, never exploited). Not agent laziness: with that menu, E1 was fixed-pose-solvable — every contract answerable from the spawn pose. The world had a discoverable pose space; the contracts never priced it; rational agents ignored it.

Both findings are one lesson — contracts are the curriculum — and they retired the menu. The clean-slate audit (TRACKA_CLEANSLATE_EVAL.md) kept every expensive asset (episode/replica machinery, CRPS + honest sigma, budgets, barrier, the baseline-actor pattern) and replaced only the menu with section 2's ladder: L1 prices the pose space, L4D separates law-learning from point-matching, and skill normalization reports a baseline-saturated contract as ~0 instead of hiding it in the scale (round-1 P1 had looked "nearly skill-free" for exactly that reason).
