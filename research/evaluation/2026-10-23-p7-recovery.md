**P7 recovery comparison — standing evaluator, 23 October 2026**

The complete six-case experiment does not support restoration of P7's original internal feature after the specified interior pulse. The untouched and exterior-only cases retain that feature; the interior and interior-plus-exterior cases regrow a different internal pattern. The two boundary-pulse cases have censoring problems and cannot establish selective failure of recovery. My decision is to stop this fixed recovery comparison and its proposed conditional boundary-restoration claim, preserving the earlier finding of persistent visible organization.

P7 is a previously selected pattern in an eight-field simulation on a periodic two-dimensional domain. Its accepted distinguishing feature is a curled A1 stripe, a low-A1 bay inside that curl, and an adjacent A0-rich band. A1 is shown in cream and A0 in cyan. The feature is recognizable within the fixed interior readout without using outer-contour closure. Gray pixels mark locations outside that readout; they are not a material boundary.

All six cases start from the same accepted full checkpoint at source time 250. A pulse resets only A0 and A1 to the model vacuum in its specified region; the six other fields remain. Each case then runs for 250 simulation units, with 101 saved observations at spacing 2.5, using one common new noise branch. These are paired interventions on one candidate, not six independent replicates or a restart of the historical random sequence. The predeclared recovery requirement is the original curled stripe, bay and adjacent band at **every one of the 21 saved observations from relative time 200 through 250**.

The distinction between D/DC and the censored cases matters. In D/DC the wider context retains an apparent enclosure at the original location, while the readable interior contains a different A1 organization. There is no observed displacement of the named curl and bay that would explain their absence from the readout. The negative finding is the failure to restore that specified organization, not disappearance of all activity or failure of an outline to close. In B, the required feature crosses the readout boundary. In DB, the original enclosure's continuation is ambiguous amid spot reorganization. Under the fixed protocol, those ambiguities remain censored; wider context cannot turn an unreadable interior into a recovery verdict.

The required causal signature was retention in S/B/C, recovery in D/DC, and clear nonrecovery in DB. It is not present. D and DC already fail the recovery requirement, and B and DB additionally prevent a clean selective boundary comparison. Thus the result supplies a limited negative for original-feature restoration after this pulse, while leaving the claimed conditional contribution of the boundary-region intervention unestablished. It does not demonstrate a membrane or an indispensable boundary function.

The strongest positive evidence remains the persistent, distinctive internal arrangement in S and C, together with the accepted historical P7 observations. Under unchanged rubric v0.1, the earlier three supported gates—localized coherent appearance, a distinguishing spatial feature, and its sampled persistence with an apparent individual—remain supported. The new S/C continuation is consistent with that finding. Recovery is additional evidence about maintenance, not a new universal gate. This experiment does not establish recovery-based maintenance, causal functional dependence, autonomy or copying of the distinguishing feature.
