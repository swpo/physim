# One P7 enclosure retains its internal organization

23 October 2026 · Assessment by the standing organism evaluator · Rubric v0.1

**One selected P7 enclosure supports all three organism-candidate gates at the level of visible organization and sampled apparent identity.** Its closed outer contour and specified internal pattern remain recognizable in all 201 retained observations from simulation time 250 to 750. No feature loss, boundary loss or identity ambiguity was observed. Active maintenance remains untested.

This updates the persistence judgment in the [initial calibration](2026-10-23.md) using additional retained evidence under the [unchanged rubric](rubric-v0.1.md). It establishes an organism-like candidate for further study, with the scope below; it does not establish a self-maintaining organism.

Before inspecting the intervening frames, I froze one enclosure and one feature at time 250: a closed outer A1 contour containing a separate curled A1 stripe with a low-A1 bay and an intermediate A0-rich band. The selected object is about one-third across and one-fifth down in the full-domain view. Its legacy label, rank 5 at that snapshot, is not a persistent identity label. Smooth deformation and intensity changes were allowed; replacement by another survivor was not.

The review covers every available subsequent movie sample at a cadence of 2.5 simulation units, using full-domain A0/A1 context and the same fixed detail crops. The 500-unit interval tests later persistence after the organization already exists. It is not a formation test or a calibrated universal lifetime threshold.

The target is mostly stationary while nearby small patterns change. A stable patterned island remains a viable interpretation. The trajectory is a fresh noisy CPU realization initialized with dressed pokes, not a reconstruction of the original archived GPU run. Ancestry before time 250, confinement, resource dependence, perturbation recovery and replication were not evaluated. Selection was retrospective: snapshots at times 25, 250 and 750 had already been seen. This is evidence about one selected candidate, not a blinded success rate or prevalence estimate. [Per-sample judgments](../../docs/assets/research/2026-10-23-p7-persistence/visual-observations.json), the [assessment](../../docs/assets/research/2026-10-23-p7-persistence/assessment.json) and [provenance](../../docs/assets/research/2026-10-23-p7-persistence/provenance.json) preserve the scope and bindings.

My next priority is to **design one bounded test of whether the boundary helps restore this internal feature after local disturbance**. Compare the same interior disturbance with an intact boundary, a selective boundary lesion, and matched damage away from the boundary, with sham controls. Freeze the perturbation, recovery window and feature criterion before running anything.

- Selective recovery with the boundary intact would support boundary-dependent maintenance and justify advancing this candidate. It would still not establish autonomy.
- Equal recovery regardless of boundary condition, or no recovery, would leave visual persistence supported but maintenance unsupported. Keep this case as a visual benchmark and redirect priority without automatic retuning.
- An ambiguous or invalid comparison would remain insufficient evidence and end that proposal without a parameter sweep.

This recommendation concerns the strength of the interpretation; it adds no fourth essential gate. No new simulation or compute has been started. The prepared feedback experiment remains held while this design decision is considered.
