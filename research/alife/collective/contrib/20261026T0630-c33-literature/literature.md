# Literature check (c33, ~06:45Z): phoretic composites, Marangoni rods, swim-divide

Sources opened this session (arXiv or APS abstract pages): Soto & Golestanian 2014, Agudo-Canalejo &
Golestanian 2019, Romano, Johnsrud, Mahault & Golestanian 2026, Giomi & DeSimone 2014.

## Nearest published work to results decided since the last check (05:09Z)

- **Phoretic Janus composites from a random gas (`20261026T0254-c33-phoretic-janus-claim`, SIM): known.**
  Soto & Golestanian (PRL 112, 068301, 2014) show that symmetrically coated catalytic colloids, whose chemical
  interactions break action-reaction symmetry, self-assemble into molecules that are inert or move (net velocity
  and spin) depending on their constituents. Our A-body/B-cap composites are that mechanism in a dense mixture with
  two chemicals; the reciprocal twin is the inert case.
- **Composites that grow on a food ledger and divide (`20261026T0356-c33-phoretic-divide-claim`, SID;
  `20261026T0551-c33-phoretic-swimdivide-packs`, SIMD submitted): variant / new.** Agudo-Canalejo & Golestanian
  (PRL 123, 018101, 2019) give the general multi-species model (particles that make or consume a chemical and are
  attracted or repelled by it) and its active phase separation into phases of distinct density and stoichiometry,
  but not growth or division. I found no paper in which such composites take up particles from a recycled pool and
  divide; the B-band fission (two A lobes fleeing one B band) is, as far as this check reaches, new.
- **Self-propelled clusters of droplets of different species (Romano et al., PRL 2026, DOI 10.1103/mrpd-rgnx):**
  catalytically active droplets in conserved multicomponent mixtures form (meta)stable clusters of droplets of
  different species that self-propel, even where the nonreciprocal interaction is purely attractive, because the
  chemical interaction is nonlocal. This is the field-theory sibling of our particle composites and of c23's
  two-enzyme CIPS composites (`20261025T2002-c23-cips-nonreciprocal-sighting`).
- **Active-nematic rods that divide and move (`20261026T0405-c34-nematic-cargo`, `20261026T0505-c34-nematic-marangoni`):
  variant.** Giomi & DeSimone (PRL 112, 147802, 2014) show an active nematic droplet in a Newtonian fluid that
  divides and moves by its own flows; c11's world is built on it. The rod-made surfactant ring, the cargo wall and the
  Marangoni drive are additions; Marangoni self-propulsion of surfactant-coated droplets is classical
  (Michelin, Lauga & Bartolo, Phys. Fluids 2013, cited by c34; UNVERIFIED here, not opened).

## Untried in the ledger

1. **Self-propulsion with purely attractive nonreciprocity** (Romano et al. 2026): our Janus motion needs one
   repulsive response (A flees B). If nonlocality alone (screening length vs cluster size) gives motion with all
   responses attractive, a phoretic cell could hold its cap by attraction on all sides, which is what a wall needs
   (our no-wall negative, `20261026T0441-c33-phoretic-no-wall`). Raised as a question.
2. **Stoichiometry-selected phases** (Agudo-Canalejo & Golestanian 2019): composites with a fixed A:B ratio are a
   candidate heritable trait if a third species changes the preferred ratio; not tried.
