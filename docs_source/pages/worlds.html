A world is a set of field dynamics. A prepared instance fixes where an experiment begins and how its instruments are arranged.

Fields assign a value to every point on a two-dimensional periodic grid. Local interactions, diffusion, and ongoing disturbances evolve those values. A “blob” is a persistent localized structure in the fields. Its movement and interactions emerge from the dynamics. Read more about field dynamics.

Worlds prepared for evaluation

Three worlds currently have validated preparations and prediction suites. They expose different physical behaviors through the same experimental interface. The registry also preserves a wider collection of world definitions and generation history.

BF: a trail that changes later motion

BF has one activator and three channels. Its trail channel stays where it is written and relaxes slowly. A brief source pulse into that channel changes the activator's later trajectory, long after the pulse ends. Removing the bilinear feedback term eliminates this effect in paired simulations. The preparation gives an agent a persistent physical memory to discover and use when predicting delayed or combined interventions.

XV: an orbiting pair held together by coupling

XV has two activators and four channels. In the prepared pair, one activator sweeps around a much less mobile partner. Pulses into either activator change the subsequent readings. Longer control simulations show that removing the cross-coupling lets the pair separate and reduces its rotation. The prediction suite probes response strength, timing, and the influence of one partner on the other.

These observations come from fresh simulations and mechanism controls for the selected preparations. See the BF and XV scientific report for figures, measurements, and model pilots.

What the instruments reveal

Two movable probe arrays sample every anonymous port at multiple positions. The number of ports depends on the preparation, as shown above. A global sensor reports each port's spatial mean and variance. A fixed source injects through one port at a time. Moving a probe changes its readings without moving the emitter or disturbing the fields.

Each trial restores the same prepared fields and apparatus, then draws independent ongoing noise. The investigator can therefore compare treatments and repeat experiments, while still needing to represent uncertainty.

A wider space of physical systems

World generation changes the number of activators and channels, their timescales, diffusion, and couplings. Earlier small systems provide interpretable examples of persistent spots, motion, binding, and transport. Larger evolved systems combine several kinds of structure. These phenomena belong to particular parameter settings; they are not properties of every world.
