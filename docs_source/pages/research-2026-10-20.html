Research update · 20 October 2026

A pattern can persist because of its surroundings, its internal interactions, or both. Three comparisons probe those dependencies: restoring an initial surrounding signal did not stop branching; removing protective action caused activity loss; and removing the resource charge did not rescue production-off activity in a third preparation.

These are controlled experiments on spatial fields, in distinct preparations with 19 or 20 fields. The bright regions are localized field activity. Splitting shapes and sustained activity are useful observations; they do not, by themselves, establish reproduction or autonomous life.

Does restoring the initial surroundings prevent branching?

We selected one already-existing pattern from a larger world and kept its local state and a four-unit neighborhood identical across four preparations. The whole-world control retained its surroundings. The isolated control replaced the exterior with the established bath. Two further arms restored only the exterior signal X₁ at the starting instant: either its original spatial profile or a uniform value with the same signed spatial mean.

Here a “split” is a sampled threshold-component event: normalized A > 0.45, periodic four-connectivity, and robust component area at least 2. The single tracked original gives way to two robust components that each overlap its previous sampled mask. This operational definition does not assign material ancestry.

The saved initial X₁ profile was insufficient to prevent branching in this preparation. Both restoration arms first split in the next sampled interval after the isolated control. The sampling does not establish an exact 2.5-unit delay, and equal intervals do not imply equal trajectories or that spatial gradients are irrelevant.

X₁ inhibits A in these equations, but its future production still depends on the evolving A field. Restoring X₁ at time zero does not restore the exterior A sources that helped maintain it in the whole world. The result therefore leaves continued environmental influence, other exterior fields, and internal branching mechanisms unresolved.

The original remained in the high-Q state at every sample where its identity was still defined. At the first split, tracking of that original ended permanently; later components were not assigned its identity or phase. This is evidence about sampled geometry, not a demonstration of material lineage, inheritance, or reproduction.

What happens when protective action is removed?

A separate comparison began with two copies of an already-active, 20-field preparation, with matched initial fields and noise-generator state. One kept a protective action enabled. The other changed only its coefficient from 2 to 0, removing P’s protective effect on an adverse signal. The declared P-production law and the external source conditions remained in place.

The intact preparation retained eligible activity at all 31 sampled times and all three thresholds, 0.30, 0.45 and 0.60. With protective action removed, the first sampled absence of eligible cores at all three thresholds was time 85. The action-off arm also met the fixed late-loss criterion over times 125–150, including no raw primary-threshold activity at 150.

This supports dependence on the engineered protective-action term under these conditions. Keeping the production law does not mean that the amount actually produced was equal between the two evolving systems. It also does not establish reciprocal resource payment or exclusive mediation by this pathway.

The intact primary active area grew from 88.5 to 599.25 spatial units² and ended with 13 eligible components. Persistence here includes substantial growth. Bounded size, recovery from collapse, and autonomy have not been demonstrated.

A subsequent audit checked the saved evidence using an independent component reconstruction and found all 1,456 contracted consistency comparisons passing. These are correlated checks of the same experiment, not 1,456 independent replications or a new dynamics run. Intermediate U₀ concentration fields at times 5–120 were not saved, so those frames’ masks could be checked geometrically but could not be re-thresholded from the missing concentrations. Conditions and action histories between saved states remain supported by the original run’s diagnostics; the audit does not independently reconstruct those full trajectories.

Does removing the resource charge rescue production-off activity?

A third comparison used a distinct 19-field, source-supported preparation. It separated two genome coefficients: protective-signal production β and the associated resource debit γ. Three paths began from the same complete state, including stored signal and noise-generator state. Only the listed coefficients differed afterward.

Absence here means no raw A above each threshold, 0.075, 0.1 and 0.125. Late samples were fixed at 125, 130, 135, 140, 145 and 150. No component-size filter is used to declare absence, and the result does not mean every field is exactly zero.

Removing production caused late activity loss while retaining the payment law. Removing the payment law as well did not restore late activity. This supports conditional dependence on the production coefficient in this preparation. The two production-off paths differ in their sampled loss brackets; shared late absence does not imply that payment has no effect, nor identify an exact five-unit delay.

Fixed γ preserves the formula γZY, not the amount actually paid or the resource trajectory. Integrated mean payment was approximately 0.12779 in I and 0.03237 in P. The contrast includes later feedback; it does not isolate a direct signal effect under matched future resource histories. The missing fourth arm also leaves payment removal with production enabled untested.

Intact primary active area grows from 203 to 817 spatial units². Persistence is therefore compatible with expansion, and bounded maintenance or autonomy remains unproved. All 93 raw activity frames were retained and replayed on the worker. Full fields are available at the common start and three endpoints, with selected chemistry at time 25; all-step reaction and chemistry histories remain producer aggregates rather than a fully saved trajectory.

What these results leave open

The present surroundings model has no exterior-only signal-production switch that preserves target production, so isolating continued exterior influence needs a different admissible control. Separately, a bounded functional reference is needed before a recovery test can distinguish maintenance from renewed expansion. The production/payment comparison narrows one mechanism question, but does not meet those requirements.

Each result concerns one selected preparation and a finite sampled horizon. The preparations differ across the three comparisons. None establishes population generality, numerical convergence, continuous-time persistence, or autonomous reproduction.

Evidence and display conventions

All three videos render completed, reviewed simulation outputs. Rendering advances no dynamics. Recorded frames are held on screen at five simulation time units per video second, with a two-second hold on the final frame. No intermediate motion is synthesized. Spatial resampling uses nearest-neighbor display, and the color scale and spatial windows stay fixed.

Run settings and exact source files

The branching comparison used a 128 × 128 grid, dx = 0.5, dt = 0.02, float32 and no noise. Elapsed time zero corresponds to a source age of 150. The protection comparison used a 288 × 288 grid, dx = 0.5, dt = 0.02, float64 and noise amplitude 0.002, starting from a copied-edge preparation. The production/payment comparison used a 96 × 96 grid, dx = 0.5, dt = 0.02, float64 and no noise, from a common complete checkpoint at source age 25 with its declared source field set. All native runs verified their 52 recorded physics locks. These checks establish implementation identity, not convergence.

In the protection movie, normalization is (U₀ − u₀)/(√2 − u₀), with u₀ = −0.5897545123014584. The movie uses the saved 0.45 mask; the saved summaries retain all three thresholds. Area and count annotations are copied from those summaries.
