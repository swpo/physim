The primary score measures error in joint predictions of sensor readings. Lower is better.

A predictor returns an empirical distribution of possible trajectories. The evaluator compares it with independent physical realizations on programs fixed before grading. It never pairs forecast member 0 with truth member 0.

Why joint predictions matter

Suppose two readings are usually high together or low together. Predicting each reading's distribution correctly while making them independent loses that relationship. Marginal errors can miss this; a joint score evaluates groups of readings together.

Each group selects specific sensor coordinates across positions and times, with a fixed scale for each coordinate. Dividing by those scales makes the group dimensionless. Group definitions and scales are declared before grading and cannot be adjusted to favor a predictor.

The score

Let xi be a scaled forecast vector, yj an independent scaled truth vector, M the number of forecast members, and N the number of truths. For a group of d coordinates:

D(a, b) = √[ Σₖ (aₖ − bₖ)² / d ]
E = (1/MN) Σᵢⱼ D(xᵢ, yⱼ) − (1/2M²) Σᵢₗ D(xᵢ, xₗ)

The second term accounts for the predictor's spread. The implementation includes all ordered forecast pairs, including self-pairs. It scores the returned empirical distribution; finite sampling can therefore affect the score even for the correct stochastic process.

  1. Calculate joint energy for each predeclared group.
  2. Average groups equally within each case.
  3. Average cases equally for the primary score.

The original reference suite uses 15 programs; BF and XV use 11 each. All request 64 forecast members and use two saved independent truth realizations per program. The score is dimensionless and has no universal “percent accurate” interpretation.

Diagnostics and framework reward

Marginal continuous ranked probability score (CRPS) measures each scalar forecast distribution. It is reported in the observable's native units. Field means have field-value units; global variances have squared units, so these should not be combined as if they had the same scale.

The Verifiers integration supplies reward = 1 / (1 + error) to the framework. That convenience mapping is separate from the scientific error and should not be presented as accuracy.

What the current score can establish

Controlled predictors show sensitivity to omitted pulses, ignored probe movements, and lost spatial structure. The original reference's primary groups also have a known limitation: two programs select coarse timestamps that miss some fine switch and recovery timing. Full-array marginal diagnostics expose those errors.

Two truths per program and one preparation per world provide limited evidence about uncertainty calibration or generalization. A low aggregate score establishes performance on the selected measurements; it does not by itself show that an agent recovered all of the underlying physics.
